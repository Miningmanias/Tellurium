# Stutter with Distant Horizons, Voxy and fast flight — 2026-10-06

## What was measured

A singleplayer client on the reference host (RTX 5070 Ti, 24 logical cores, 8 GB
heap, G1), Minecraft 1.21.1 on NeoForge, fresh world of seed 0 each run, with
ScalableLux. The player is moved by the development "player tour"
(`mod/common/.../bench/PlayerTour`), and `StutterProbe` reports for the
minute after the first 8 seconds of the session:

- **late ticks**: a server tick is due every 50 ms; a tick that ends more than
  75 ms (or 150 ms) after the one before was held up;
- **long frames**: the client's frame times, sampled about every 2 ms, over 33
  ms and over 100 ms;
- **collector pauses**: stop-the-world collections over 20 ms. In singleplayer
  the client and the server share the process, so a pause stops both;
- what the server thread was doing while a tick was late.

Three scenes (`build/demo/measure.sh` in the working tree, not in the
repository; the settings are in the commands below):

| Scene | Mods | Player |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distant Horizons | Distant Horizons 3.3.3 | stands at y 230 and turns |
| Voxy | Voxy 0.2.16-beta through Roxy 0.3.3, Sodium 0.8.12 (NeoForge 21.1.255) | stands at y 230 and turns |
| Flight | the same as Voxy | flies at 240 blocks/s at y 170 |

One run per figure unless a range is given; runs of the same configuration
differ by a few late ticks.

So Voxy got faster and smoother; flight got smooth; Distant Horizons got much
smoother at about 70% of the chunks per minute. The Distant Horizons figure is
a choice, described below.

For comparison, the Distant Horizons scene without this mod: 0 late ticks, no
frame over 33 ms, 0.9 s of collector time, and far fewer chunks (449 chunks/s
in its own pregeneration; `docs/evidence/comparison-distant-horizons.md`).

## What caused the stutter

1. **Far terrain started before the GPU was ready.** The bridges asked for
   hundreds of chunks as soon as a player was in the world, while the GPU
   kernels were still being built. All of them were generated by the original
   code on every core, and the chunk the player stood in or flew into waited
   behind them: on NeoForge the server waits for the chunk a moving entity
   enters (`Entity.setPosRaw`), which was 1.8 to 9.3 s of the late time in the
   flight runs.
2. **Too much under way at once.** The Voxy bridge kept up to 682 chunks in
   progress (heap / 12 MiB). Every pause of the collector copies what is live;
   with that many chunks alive the median pause was 28 ms, with 192 it was 14
   ms, and the area filled only 4% slower.
3. **Far terrain competing with travel.** At 240 blocks/s the game needs a few
   hundred chunks a second ahead of the player. With the Voxy generation off
   the flight had 4 late ticks; with it on, 22 to 89.
4. **Distant Horizons' own work.** In its scene most of the processor went to
   Distant Horizons' threads: its rough surface generator, which evaluates the
   world generator's density down every other column (about 4.6 cores), and
   the lighting, storing and building of what comes back. Their garbage, not
   this mod's chunks, set the collector's pace: limiting how many chunks stay
   loaded changed nothing, limiting how many of Distant Horizons' requests are
   in progress changed everything.
5. An idle unload started just before a tick was due made that tick a few
   milliseconds late: the median gap in flight was 53 ms instead of 50.

## What was changed

- `compat/LodPace`: the bridges start nothing while a level's kernels are
  being built; with nobody online nothing is held back (a pregeneration from
  the console runs flat out); otherwise the number of chunks or requests they keep in progress follows
  the server's ticks (a late tick takes 30% of the allowance, on-time ticks
  give it back over about 12 s), the collector (a pause of 25 ms or more takes
  15%), and the players' speed (from 30 blocks/s on the allowance is 37.5%).
- Voxy bridge: at most 256 chunks in progress by default instead of up to 1024,
  and Voxy's queue is allowed 512 sections instead of 8,192 while the
  allowance is cut.
- Distant Horizons bridge: in a client at most 8 of its requests are in
  progress at once, rough-surface requests included
  (`tellurium.dh.activeTiles`; 64 on a dedicated server); the rest wait
  their turn. Tiles are started ahead outwards from the player as well as
  around the last request.
- `compat/DistantHorizonsRoughSpeedup`: the density function the rough
  generator evaluates has its per-column parts (the game's `cache_2d` and
  `flat_cache` marks, 1,788 of them in the vanilla Overworld) remember their
  last column per thread, after checking on test columns that the part does
  not vary with height. The values are the same; the rough generator does the
  same work with less of the processor. Its effect on how fast distant terrain
  appears was not measured (there is no figure for it in the log).
- `MinecraftServerIdleUnloadMixin`: no idle unload within 4 ms of the next tick.

## The start of a session (2026-10-06, later)

The figures above leave out the first 8 seconds (`tellurium.bench.tourDelay=8`). Measured from the
moment the player joins instead, one run each before these changes: Voxy 5 late ticks and 4 frames over
33 ms in the minute, Distant Horizons 12 to 17 and 10 to 13, and Flight, where the player is thrown into
ungenerated terrain at 240 blocks/s in the first half second, 13 late ticks with one gap of 1.4 s.

In that flight run every worker thread was inside this mod's biome gathering (the 6x6 quart columns a
chunk's surface rules can read) for the first burst of far chunks, while the server waited for the
player's own chunk. Three changes:

- Biome gathering asks for each neighbouring chunk once and reads the biome containers section by section,
  instead of going through the chunk's height and status checks for each of 3,456 quarts.
- When the first player joins, far terrain starts at a quarter of its allowance and has all of it about
  nine seconds later. How fast Voxy's area fills did not change measurably (5,392 chunks 9 s after joining
  and 56,460 after 39 s; 6,415 and 55,113 before).
- Building the kernels of a later dimension no longer blocks submissions for one that is ready (the device's
  monitor was held for the whole build).

Afterwards, from the moment of joining: Flight 5 to 8 late ticks, still with one gap of 1.0 to 1.9 s at
the very start, Voxy 8 late ticks (one run). So the burst is gone from the thread dumps and the stall is
not: with the workers idle, the server waits about a second for the first fully generated chunk of a
session that is one second old (cold code, a chain of stages for one chunk). A player cannot fly at that
speed half a second after joining; the tour can. From 8 s on the three scenes are where they were, within
run-to-run differences (Flight 6 late ticks / 0 long frames, Voxy 2 / 1, Distant Horizons 21 / 19).

## Less garbage per chunk (2026-10-07)

An allocation profile of the Voxy scene (Java Flight Recorder, dumped while the area filled): 19% of
everything allocated was the game rebuilding the world generator's density functions for a chunk
(`NoiseChunk` wraps the whole noise router, `mapAll`), 12% section encoding for saves, 9% reading chunks
back from disk that had been unloaded half-generated. The mod already put the first off until a function is
used, which for a chunk whose terrain came from the GPU is never; but the constructor then adds the
structure term to the final density, building that sum asks for the final density's bounds, and answering
needs the rebuild. The sum is now built on first use as well (`LazyMappedDensity.sum`). In the profile of
the server benchmark afterwards the rebuilding is 6.5% of allocation: what is left is the climate sampler
of the biome step and height queries by structures.

What that shows: generation is 6 to 10% faster where it runs flat out (server pregeneration, Distant
Horizons' pregeneration). In the client scenes the collector has about a tenth less to do in Flight and
Voxy, where late ticks and long frames were already near none, and nothing got faster: the Voxy fill is
limited by the processor, which Voxy's own threads share (36% of the samples), and the flight by the
allowance. The Distant Horizons scene does **not** show an effect that can be told from its own spread:
sessions of the same configuration fall into a calmer kind (15 to 17 late ticks, 3.0 s of collector time)
and a rougher kind (24 to 39, 3.7 to 4.5 s), three of four rough with the change off and two of five with
it on. The rough sessions are not the ones with more chunks loaded (they have fewer at the end, so more
were saved inside the minute); what decides the kind is not known.

Tried and not adopted: more requests in progress for Distant Horizons (12 instead of 8: 47 late ticks and
no more chunks, 32,416 against 33,088), and a larger allowance for far terrain while flying
(`tellurium.lod.travelling`, default 0.375: 0.5 changed nothing measurable; 0.75 gave 965 and 991 chunks/s
against 794 to 852, with 6 and 12 late ticks against 2 to 6 and one long frame per run).

Seen and left: in the server benchmark a tenth of all allocation is the game copying its map of loaded
chunks every time the set changes (`ChunkMap.promoteChunkMap`). Other threads read that copy, so replacing
it is a change to the chunk system itself; it is 0.2% in the Voxy scene.

## Not done, not known

- Only 1.21.1 on NeoForge was measured. The code is common to every build, but
  none of this was run on 1.21.11, on Fabric, or on another machine or heap
  size; the allowances were chosen on an 8 GB heap.
- The start of a session is still rougher than the rest: until the kernels are
  built (1 to 4 s after the server starts) the game's own chunks are generated
  by the original code.
- A dedicated server with players online was not measured. Distant Horizons'
  pregeneration from the console of the installed server, nobody online, was
  re-timed after these changes: 66,049 chunks at 2,040 and 2,023 chunks/s
  (2,041 to 2,119 before), and the column-writer check again compared
  16,777,216 columns with 0 differences. With the pacing applied there too it
  had dropped to 1,751, which is why it is not.
- The collector's pauses are still the largest part of what is left; the mod
  cannot choose the collector or the heap.
- Real play (a person flying with an elytra) was not recorded; the tour moves
  the player by teleporting 12 blocks every tick.
